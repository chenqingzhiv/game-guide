# Moonlight Peaks: Money Making Guide

![Moonlight Peaks money-making loop from field to Keg, Jam Maker and coins](img/money-flow-overview.webp)

Money makes Moonlight Peaks move — and it's the system players most often get wrong. Plant too much and you water yourself into a stamina crater; sell raw and you leave half your profit on the floor. This guide walks through every income source in the game, stage by stage, from your first ten nights to the day you stop worrying about coins entirely.

## The Golden Rule: Process Before You Sell

The single biggest mistake new players make is selling produce straight from the field. Raw crops sell for pocket change. Processed goods sell for multiples — a Keg full of wine routinely pays **8–17×** the raw grape value, and a cooking dish can more than double the ingredients that went into it. Almost every serious money-maker in the game is a machine on your farm, not a crop in your hands.

**Second rule: keep 1–2 of everything.** Recipes, quests and the museum all demand items you'll have sold if you weren't careful. Never sell your last strawberry, your last fish, or your last piece of ore — the [Museum & Nokturna guide](museum-nokturna.md) explains exactly what you'll need to keep.

## Stage 1: First Ten Nights (Beginner)

You start broke and rusty-tooled. That's fine — the first ten nights are about building the habits and the machines that make everything after it profitable.

![Moonlight Peaks early-game income sources from foraging, fishing and Wild Potatoes](img/early-game-cash.webp)

### Nights 1–3: Seed Money
- **Wild Potatoes** (3-night growth, 20g seeds) are the perfect starter cash crop — plant a small field and harvest twice before the first week is out.
- **Forage everything.** Flowers, mushrooms and dig-spots respawn every night. Sparkly swirls can hide **Diamonds (2,000g)** or rare recipes — check every glowing spiral you pass.
- **Follow the quests.** The early quests ("The Need for Herbs", "A Roof Over Your Head") hand out recipes, blueprints and coins for work you'd be doing anyway.

### Nights 4–6: First Machines
- Finish **Orlock's Wine Scheme** to unlock the **Keg** blueprint — the most important machine in the game costs just **20 Wood**.
- Start **Copper tool upgrades** (1,000g + 3 Copper Bars each) at Ridge's Howling Hammer. Priority order: **Pickaxe → Axe → Watering Can** (see the [Tool Upgrade Guide](tools-upgrades.md)).

### Nights 7–10: First Goals
- Build **3 Kegs** and start processing grapes.
- Save toward the **Barn (4,000g)** — the best early investment, because it produces **fertilizer**, and fertilizer is what raises your crop stars and therefore your wine price.

> **First-ten-night target: 5,000–10,000g** in the bank, 3 Kegs built, and a copper pickaxe.

## Stage 2: Spring — The Wine Economy

Spring is when Moonlight Peaks teaches you the one skill that funds the whole game: **never sell a grape again.**

### The crop ladder
Normal-water players should lean on the season's best direct sellers while they unlock magic:

| Crop | Season | Profit per plant |
|------|:------:|:----------------:|
| Carrot | Spring | ~160g+ |
| Skunktail | Summer | ~180g |
| Pumpkin | Autumn | ~220g |

### Kegs, not raw sales
| Machine | Makes | Profit vs raw |
|---------|-------|:-------------:|
| **Keg** | White Wine (4 White Grapes) | ~460g per batch |
| **Keg** | Red Wine (4 Blood Grapes) | ~360g per batch |
| **Keg** | Beer (3 Wily Wheat) | ~420g per batch |
| **Keg** | Mana Wine (2W + 2B + 1 Mana Essence) | ~3× Red Wine |

**White Wine outsells Red**, so favour White Grapes early. Wine profit scales 8–17× with star quality — which is exactly why the Barn (fertilizer) comes before you scale up.

![Moonlight Peaks Keg and processing profit multipliers](img/keg-wine-economy.webp)

### What to buy first with your first 10,000g
1. **Barn (4,000g)** + a **Cheeken (1,200g)** — eggs feed the cooking economy and fertilizer feeds everything.
2. **Jam Maker (40 Wood + 20 Stone + 10 Iron Bars)** — turns fruit + Sugar into jams once you have the Mill.
3. **Mill (8,000g)** — turns Sugarbone into Sugar, the gateway to the jam economy. A big early spend; the compounding starts after it.
4. **Fruit tree** (2,000–6,000g) — long-term passive income (see Stage 5).

## Stage 3: Summer — Magic Crops and Scaling

Summer is when the magical crops arrive, and they're not subtle about it.

### Best crops for profit
| Rank | Crop | Season | Profit per plant |
|:----:|------|:------:|:----------------:|
| 🥇 | **Gobbler** ⚡ | Summer | ~800g |
| 🥈 | **Drikker** ⚡ | Spring/Summer | ~660g |
| 🥉 | **Sweet Wicca** ⚡ | Winter | ~600g |
| 4 | **Happy Mandrake** ⚡ | Autumn | ~520g |
| 5 | **Black Sun Currant** ⚡ | Winter | ~380g |

*⚡ = requires the Aquaflux spell to water.*

**Gobbler is the best crop in the game** — ~800g per plant when you feed each one a fish or bug while it grows. Unlock the **Mana Extractor** (30 Stone + 10 Iron Bars, blueprint from Blood Grape Stomping at the Spring 24 festival) so your magical crops turn into **Mana Essence** instead of raw sales — Mana Wine and Enchanted tools both want it.

### Cooking for profit
Cooking is a money engine the raw-sale players ignore. The margins are real:

| Dish | Ingredients | Cooked value | Boost vs raw |
|------|-------------|:------------:|:------------:|
| Grilled Wild Potato | 2 Wild Potato | 30g | ❌ −25% (sell raw!) |
| Moonfruit Juice | 1 Moonfruit | 45g | ✅ +50% |
| Hearty Stew | 2 Potato + 1 Carrot + meat | 120g | ✅ +50% |
| **Cheeken Pot Pie** | Egg + Wheat + veg | 110g | ✅ **+175%** |
| **Draculamb Roast** | Milk + meat + 2 herbs | 350g | ✅ **+190%** |
| Moon Crystal Cake | Moon Crystal + Wheat + Egg | 500g | ✅ +150% |

**Rule of thumb:** never cook a dish that sells for less than its ingredients. Grilled Wild Potato is a trap; Cheeken Pot Pie and Draculamb Roast are printers. (Full tables live in the [Cooking & Recipes Guide](cooking-recipes.md).)

### The Cheese Press
The first time you milk a **Pig Goat** (3,500g) or **Draculamb** (4,500g), Luna mails you the **Cheese Press** blueprint. Milk → cheese is a big price jump over raw milk, and it's a pure profit button on animals you already own.

## Stage 4: Autumn — Festivals

Autumn's harvest festivals are the easiest coins in the game — and the prizes are worth far more than the cash.

| Event | Date | Prize for winning |
|-------|:----:|-------------------|
| Harvest Festival — Crop Judging | Fall 16 | **Golden Watering Can** (waters 5×5) or 10,000g runner-up |
| Harvest Festival — Cooking | Fall 16 | **Kitchen Extension** plan or 8,000g |
| Harvest Festival — Hay Bale Maze | Fall 16 | 2,000g (under 60s) or **Horse Mount** (under 30s) |
| Harvest Festival — Nokturna | Fall 16 | **Legendary Card Pack** |

Hoard your **highest-quality crops** from Spring onwards — the Crop Judging Grand Competition wants your best five (one each of veg, fruit, magic, flower, forage). A single Golden Watering Can win is worth more than a season of raw sales.

**Save 15,000–20,000g for the Festival of Eternal Night (Winter 13)** — the Night Market sells **Mana Shards** (+10 Max Mana, limit 3) that you can't get anywhere else.

## Stage 5: Winter & Year 2 — Passive Income and Enchanted Tools

The late game is about turning a working farm into an engine you barely touch.

### Enchanted tools are the endgame unlock
Gold tools are good; **Enchanted tools** are a different species. Buy a gold tool, wait 6 days, then enchant it for **48,000g + the gold tool + 5 Mana Essence** — every swing is **zero stamina**. Do your pickaxe first: free mining is effectively free money, since ore respawns.

### Passive income stacks
- **Fruit trees** — no watering, ~25 nights to mature, then fruit every 6–8 nights forever. The ultimate set-and-forget investment.
- **Barn animals** — eggs and milk convert into cooked dishes and cheese. A happy herd is a daily income.
- **Beehives** — honey is a quiet steady earner (just keep red flowers away from your Draculambs).
- **Mining** — the Cave of Echoes respawns; once your tools are Enchanted, a nightly cave run is pure profit.

### The Antique Clock
Collect **100 Soul Blobs** (night-only bug catches) and trade them to Death for the **Antique Clock** — every night becomes **25 minutes** long. More night is more everything: more crops watered, more ore mined, more fish caught. Start collecting the moment you unlock bug catching (around Night 12).

### Year 2 targets
- **10+ Kegs** running continuously (wine + Mana Wine).
- **Greenhouse** (win Spring 11's Crop Display Contest) for year-round crops.
- **Moon Crystal Cake** (Cooking Level 8) as your EXP buff and a 500g sale.
- **Golden Feast** (Cooking Level 10) — full restore, 1,200g, and all buffs for emergencies.

> **Year 2+ target: 100,000g+** liquid, and the only real bottleneck left is stamina — which Enchanted tools and cooking have already solved.

## Daily Routine for Maximum Gold

1. **Wake, cast Aquaflux** to water everything (then Ethereal Hands once you have it — one cast harvests 32 crops).
2. **Harvest and process first** — load the Kegs, Jam Makers and Cheese Press before anything else.
3. **Pet and feed the animals.**
4. **Mine or fish** the rest of the night — ore respawns and fish are steady coins.
5. **Check the quest board and mailbox** — quests are free money.
6. **End the night by planting** whatever you just bought (plant on a **Full Moon** — everything grows 50% faster).

## FAQ

**Q: What's the fastest early money?**
Foraging + fishing + Wild Potatoes in the first week, then Kegs the moment you can. A 5,000–10,000g bank by Night 10 is very doable.

**Q: Sell raw or process?**
Process. Wine (8–17×), jams, cheese and the right dishes all beat raw sales by wide margins. The only exception: dishes like Grilled Wild Potato that sell for less than their ingredients.

**Q: When should I build the Barn?**
As soon as you can afford it (4,000g) — it produces fertilizer, and fertilizer raises the star quality of everything in your processing chain. Barn first, animals second.

**Q: What's the best long-term money-maker?**
Grapes → Wine in the Keg, backed by fruit trees (no upkeep at all). Add fertilizer for star quality and Mana Wine once you have Mana Essence.

**Q: Is mining worth it for money?**
Indirectly, yes — ore respawns, smelts into bars that fund tool upgrades, and once your tools are Enchanted, a cave run is pure profit. Just don't sell copper/iron casually; you need it constantly.

**Q: What should I spend my first 10,000g on?**
A Barn + Cheeken, then the Jam Maker and Mill. Machines compound; decorations don't.
