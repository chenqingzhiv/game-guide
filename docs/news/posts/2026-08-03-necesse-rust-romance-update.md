## Necesse Rust & Romance Update — Full Breakdown + Automated Defense Guide

I spent most of the **Necesse** free weekend glued to my monitor, and honestly? This is the most fun I've had with a sandbox survival game all year. The **Rust & Romance update** — the biggest content drop of 2026 for the Danish studio Fair Games' genre-blending sandbox — went live on **July 30**, and it's a lot. There's a new endgame robot incursion, a dating sim hiding inside your colony management, bigger biomes, and dozens of quality-of-life changes.

This guide covers everything new in the patch, and then gets practical: I'll show you the **automated trap defense** setup that kept my settlement standing while the Scrapyard robots came knocking.

### What Is Necesse, Anyway?

If you haven't played it: **Necesse** is an action-RPG + dungeon crawler + colony simulator + survival sandbox all rolled into one. Imagine *Terraria*'s mining and crafting crossed with *RimWorld*'s settlement automation, in an infinitely generated world you can explore with friends (it supports co-op multiplayer).

- **Developer / publisher:** Fair Games ApS (Denmark)
- **Steam release (1.0):** October 16, 2025
- **Price:** $14.99 (currently 50% off — see pricing below)
- **Player rating:** 93% positive from 27,600+ reviews on Steam
- **Platforms:** PC (Steam), **Steam Deck Verified**

Your settlers can farm, mine, fish, cook, and sort loot on their own — the automation layer is the game's signature. And that's exactly what the Rust & Romance update leans into.

### The "Rust" Half: Scrapyard Incursion

The headline feature is a brand-new **endgame incursion** called the **Scrapyard**, and it breaks every rule Necesse established before it. Instead of another fantasy dungeon, you drop into a **mechanical wasteland** made of scrap metal, oil, and rusting machinery.

**What's in the Scrapyard:**

- A new biome type full of **robotic and mechanical enemies** — no more skeletons and slimes here
- **New combat mechanics** and equipment that work differently from the traditional fantasy gear
- A **unique boss** with attack patterns the community has never seen in Necesse
- **Exclusive loot**, including the **Bumper Car mount** shown off in the launch trailer — yes, you ride a literal bumper car around your world

The team designed this content specifically to go "all off-fantasy," and you can feel it. It's the first time the game's endgame genuinely surprised me since launch.

### The "Romance" Half: Settler Personalities & Relationships

The other half of the update turns your colony management into something closer to a life sim.

**Settler personalities** are now unique per villager, split into two halves:

- **Perks** — traits that improve what a settler is good at, from combat prowess to work efficiency
- **Preferences** — what each settler needs and wants; optimize for these and you get "crazy combat allies or speedy workers," per the developers

**Recreation system:** The old "work break" is replaced with actual recreation time. Settlers now independently dance near music players, go for jogs, and do other ambient social stuff. Your colony finally *feels* alive instead of acting like a conveyor belt of workers.

**Romance quests:** Here's where it gets fun. To start a relationship, you hand a settler a **flower** — the devs actually recommend queuing a few up in your inventory before you log in. That triggers a **chain of unique quests** that can end with the settler falling in love with you. It's a full relationship arc, not a one-click marriage mechanic.

### Bigger Biomes & Everything Else

Beyond the two headline features, the update is packed:

- **Expanded biomes** — world areas are noticeably bigger, which changes how you plan long-distance supply lines
- **Charred Forest** — a new mini-biome to explore
- **Onboarding revamp** — the tutorial and new-player intro were completely reworked
- **Resource transformation system** — new ways to convert materials
- **Dozens of new items:** decorations, enemies, weapons, armor sets, pets, achievements, and new music/sound effects
- **Patch 1.3.0** ships with bug fixes, balance changes, performance improvements, and updated modding tools

!!! tip "Veterans: your save is safe, but..."
    Old saves are compatible, but the developers recommend starting a new world to fully experience the biome changes and new systems. If you're mid-progression, the settler personality re-rolls and world-gen changes can make an existing settlement feel odd.

### Automated Base Defense — Stop the Scrapyard Robots Cold

Here's the practical part. With the new incursion and the game's escalating **raid pressure** (raids scale as you complete Elder quests), a good defense is the difference between "annoying cleanup" and "my villagers are dead."

I based this section on a real playthrough — the approach in the Bilibili guide video by *大叔追云彩* — where automated traps flat-out *remove* raiders before they ever touch a villager. Here's the full setup.

![Rows of traps lined up along the path into the settlement](/assets/images/necesse/necesse-trap-defenses.webp "Automated trap defenses — arrow and flame traps wired along the approach path")

**Step 1: Understand traps — you can't craft them.**

The single most important fact about Necesse defense: **traps cannot be crafted**. Every trap is a world drop you have to mine:

- **Arrow traps** and **flame traps** are found in caves at any depth
- **Void traps** are found only in the Dungeon (and need a Demonic Pickaxe or better to mine)
- **Glyph traps** (Bounce, Chicken, Reverse Damage) also come from the Dungeon

Trap quality scales with the cave tier you find them in — Stone → Deep Stone → Sandstone → Swamp Stone → Snow Stone → Granite → Ice → Obsidian → Basalt — so dig deep for the good ones.

**Trap comparison table** (stats from the Necesse wiki):

| Trap | Damage | Range | Cooldown | Effect | Found |
|------|--------|-------|----------|--------|-------|
| Arrow Trap (Stone/Wood) | 35 | 13 tiles | 1s | Fires an arrow | Forest Caves |
| Flame Trap (Stone) | 20 | 8 tiles | 1s | 2 flame bursts + **On Fire** debuff | Forest Caves |
| Dungeon Void Trap | 35 | 8 tiles | 1s | Fires a damaging beam | Dungeon |
| Bounce Glyph Trap | 0 | close | 10s | Repels + makes targets bouncy 4s | Dungeon |

**Step 2: Funnel them into a kill zone.**

Raiders prefer **open terrain > doors > walls**, so they'll path toward the easiest route in. Use that against them:

- Build a **perimeter wall** around the settlement with **one or two controlled entrances**
- Make walls **thick — 2 to 6 tiles**. Thin walls get smashed; thick walls make raiders commit to your entrance instead
- **Zig-zag hallways** with a door at each bend — each door slows the raid and gives your traps extra trigger time
- Lay **mud tiles** (from swamps) in the approach path — they slow movement, keeping enemies inside your trap range longer

![Raiders pushing toward the settlement while the player fights them off](/assets/images/necesse/necesse-raiders-attack.webp "Raiders attacking a Necesse settlement — the kill zone funnels them into the trap field")

**Step 3: Wire the traps properly.**

This is where most people go wrong. Traps sit dormant until you wire them:

- Connect **sensors** to your traps so they only fire when hostiles are within ~5 squares
- Set **timers** to **22 ticks** — a 20-tick timer (1 second) can cause traps to skip a cycle
- Use a **zig-zag double line** of traps facing each other so enemies eat damage coming and going
- **Don't set traps where villagers walk** — use an inverted zone over the kill area, or an **AND gate** wired to a lever you only flip during raids. Otherwise your own farmers become the casualties

**Step 4: Keep your guards ahead of the curve.**

Raid difficulty scales with your armor tier and progression, so:

- Keep guards equipped **one tier below your own gear** — never two
- Aim for **13–16 guards** once raids start coming regularly
- Give guards **area-of-effect or piercing weapons** (the Void Staff is a standout early-to-mid game)
- Use the **C key** for RTS-style commands to summon every settler to defend during a raid
- Build a small **3×3 pillbox** at each corner of your inner wall — if raiders break through, they still funnel into a 1-tile choke point

![Trap field activating as enemies step into the kill zone](/assets/images/necesse/necesse-trap-kill-zone.webp "The trap kill zone in action — enemies take damage the moment they enter the wired corridor")

**Step 5: Don't over-progress your raids.**

Every **Elder quest** you complete raises raid difficulty. If you're not ready, pause progression and grind gear instead — the raids will still scale to you, but the gap is much more manageable. One player's report sums it up: with six trapped halls plus a lava room, **no raider ever made it into the village.**
