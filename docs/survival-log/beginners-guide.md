## What Is Survival Log?

Survival Log is a **single-player zombie survival simulation** built around a two-act structure: a peaceful **pre-apocalypse planning phase** and a brutal **post-apocalypse survival phase**. It's been described by players as **"Project Zomboid meets This War of Mine"** — the top-down perspective and permadeath tension of the former, the small-scale, resource-counting humanity of the latter.

**The one-line pitch:** *Project Zomboid's hopelessness, but the roguelike twist means your shopping list from the last run is the character progression.*

Two design decisions make Survival Log stand out from the zombie crowd:

1. **The prep phase is the game.** Most zombie survival sims start you inside the apocalypse. Survival Log gives you a **ten-hour countdown** before it begins — and spending those hours well is harder and more interesting than the survival that follows.
2. **Every death is progress.** The game is structured as a **roguelike rebirth loop**: your character dies, and you restart with a bank of points to spend on permanent skills. Hidden stash locations and survival tricks you discovered are literally written into your in-game **Survival Log** so future runs start smarter.

## The 10-Hour Countdown: How the Prep Phase Works

The game opens on a city that's still normal. You have **ten in-game hours** until the outbreak is everywhere. Think of it as a timer-based shopping trip with existential stakes: everything you don't buy now, you'll have to scavenge later while zombies own the streets.

The Bilibili full-version let's-play splits the countdown into four natural moves, and I've found the same rhythm works on every run:

![Survival Log 10-hour countdown — supermarket run, hardware store, fortify home, final sweep](/assets/images/survival-log/survival-log-countdown.webp)

| Time window | What to do | What to grab |
|:------------|:-----------|:-------------|
| **Hours 0–2** | Supermarket run | Staple food + bottled water (canned goods, instant noodles, rice) |
| **Hours 2–4** | Hardware store | Wood planks, steel plates, screws, hammer, crowbar, melee weapons |
| **Hours 4–6** | Fortify home | Board windows, brace the door, set up obstacles, organize storage |
| **Hours 6–10** | Final sweep | Radio, gasoline, first-aid kit, iodine, gauze — then tidy the stash |

When the countdown hits zero, zombies flood the city and you're in the survival phase with your home as the base. The **home is your core base**: it's where you'll eat, sleep, farm, and defend from. The whole prep phase is about making that home self-sufficient before it matters.

## The 3-Tier Shopping List: What to Actually Buy

The single most useful thing I learned from the guide videos is that the shopping priority is tiered — and **most new players buy the wrong things**. Here's the order that works:

### Tier 1 — Survival Basics (buy first, buy a lot)
- **Food that doesn't spoil and needs no fire:** compressed biscuits, canned luncheon meat, instant noodles, rice, dried noodles, soda crackers, canned fruit. Shelf life and "eat it cold" matter more than taste.
- **Drinking water:** large mineral-water bottles, bottled water, and **water-purification tablets** (you'll use them later to treat dirty water when you venture out).
- Water shortage is brutal in Survival Log — dehydration heavily drains your health, so don't under-buy.

### Tier 2 — Home Security (your second stop)
- **Hardware store haul:** wood planks, reinforcing steel plates, screws, a hammer, and a **crowbar**.
  - The planks and steel go on windows and doors — they measurably reduce the chance zombies break in.
  - The **crowbar is essential later**: it's the tool that opens chests and stashes when you scavenge.
- **Silent melee weapons:** a baseball bat or a **fire axe**. Melee is the beginner's friend because it's quiet — it won't pull a horde onto your position the way a gunshot will.

### Tier 3 — Mid-Term Survival (the "survive week two" layer)
- **Radio** — receives outside information, warns you about incoming hordes, and unlocks rescue routes.
- **Gasoline + a portable can** — vehicle fuel for long-distance scavenging later.
- **First-aid kit, iodine tincture, gauze** — treat scratches and bites; a zombie scratch causes ongoing health drain if ignored.

### What NOT to buy (the beginner trap)
- **Guns and ammunition.** Expensive, loud, and every shot draws nearby zombies. New players who buy firearms tend to die faster, not slower.
- **Fresh vegetables and fruit.** They rot. By day five you'll be throwing them out.
- **Luxury goods.** Money is tight and storage is finite — spend on survival, not comfort.

> The devs even surfaced the philosophy through the games press: plan, don't panic. Your ten hours are a budget, and every item is a bet about what the apocalypse will take from you first.

## Post-Outbreak Survival: Safehouse Setup

Once the city falls, Survival Log becomes a **base-management survival sim**. You spend stamina to craft, cook, and research upgrades; you decide when it's safe enough to leave home and scavenge (ruined supermarkets, schools); and you juggle daily events that throw tasks and choices at you. Clear daily-event tasks to earn **survival points**, which unlock perks — for example, slowing down how fast your hunger bar empties.

![Survival Log safehouse survival checklist — reinforce, balcony farm, power, radio, meds, mind](/assets/images/survival-log/survival-log-safehouse.webp)

**The safehouse checklist that keeps runs alive past week one:**

1. **Reinforce every entrance.** Board the windows, brace the front door. The prep-phase hardware is what buys you the calm days you need to build everything else.
2. **Set up a balcony farm.** This is the game's answer to long-term food: grow crops in pots at home so you're not dependent on scavenging runs for calories. It's the "cozy" survival skill hiding inside a zombie game.
3. **Power and heat.** A generator plus stored fuel matters once winter or cold events arrive — the full-version let's-play hit a snowstorm around day 25 and the temperature check is real.
4. **Keep the radio on.** It's your early-warning system for hordes and your line to rescue routes.
5. **Build a medical kit.** Scratches and bites cause lasting health drain; iodine and gauze are the difference between a bandaged setback and a slow death.
6. **Manage your mind.** Solitude and witnessing the horror outside trigger the **mental entropy system** — anxiety events that debuff you. Writing in your journal and listening to radio broadcasts restore your mental state. It's a soft but real mechanic: a broken mind kills as surely as a bite.

**Food is currency.** One of the more surprising systems is that after the outbreak, **food becomes a tradeable resource** — the Bilibili video where the player "uses food to exchange for everything" is exactly right. You can trade with neighbors (who will happily drain your pantry, hence the community's "the neighbor is a money pit" joke) and use a **drone** for non-contact trading with distant survivors. Keep a food surplus; it's both your safety net and your wallet.

## The Rebirth Loop: Dying Is the Progression

Survival Log's roguelike structure is its most underrated feature, and the one that separates it from flat survival sims:

![Survival Log rebirth loop — prepare, outbreak, survive, die and learn, restart with permanent skills](/assets/images/survival-log/survival-log-loop.webp)

- **Death resets the run** — but you earn **points based on what you did**, which you spend before the next run on **permanent skills** that carry over.
- **The Survival Log records your discoveries.** Hidden material caches and survival techniques you found last run are written down in-game, so a new run isn't a blind restart — it's a smarter one.
- **Your starting identity changes the difficulty** (more below), which gives the loop meaningful variety even before skills kick in.
- **Endless Mode** unlocks after you survive **20+ days in Story Mode** — then you can hoard and fortify forever, which is what the dedicated "囤囤鼠" (hoarder-mouse) players live for.

The loop is why reviewers kept coming back to the "optimize every run" framing: the meta-progression gives failures a purpose, and the ten-hour prep phase becomes a speedrun of personal optimization once you know what you're doing.

## Starting Identities: Your Difficulty Dial

At the start of a run you pick an identity, and each one changes what you bring into the ten-hour countdown:

| Identity | Vibe | What it changes |
|:---------|:-----|:----------------|
| 💼 **Worker (打工仔)** | Default, balanced | Average starting money; a middle-class apartment as a base |
| 🎓 **College Student (大学生)** | Hard mode | Tight budget, smaller dorm base — but you know the campus, which has resources |
| 📦 **Warehouse Manager (仓库管理员)** | Interesting | Access/stock knowledge that can pay off in the prep and scavenge phases |

New players should start as the **Worker** — the extra money smooths out the early shopping-list mistakes. The community's consensus (and the video titles are blunt about it) is that the other identities are where the challenge and the interesting decisions live.

## Survival Log vs Project Zomboid / 7 Days to Die / This War of Mine

If you've played *Project Zomboid*, *7 Days to Die*, or *This War of Mine*, you'll recognize Survival Log's bones. Here's the honest comparison:

| Dimension | Survival Log | Project Zomboid | 7 Days to Die | This War of Mine |
|:----------|:-------------|:----------------|:--------------|:-----------------|
| **Price** | $10.99 (15% off at launch) | $19.99 | $24.99 | $19.99 |
| **Perspective** | Top-down 2D | Isometric 2D | First-person 3D | Side-scrolling 2D |
| **Pre-apocalypse prep** | ✅ The core hook — 10-hour countdown | ❌ Starts in the outbreak | ❌ Starts in the outbreak | ❌ Starts in the war |
| **Roguelike progression** | ✅ Death → permanent skills | ❌ (permadeath only) | ❌ | ⚠️ New survivors, shared shelter |
| **Base building** | Safehouse reinforcement + layout | Deep (walls, barricades) | Deep (block-based) | Shelter upgrades |
| **Mental health system** | ✅ Mental entropy + journal | ⚠️ Mood/panic | ❌ | ✅ Mood/despair |

**The honest verdict:** if you want a deep, long-haul zombie sandbox you'll play for a hundred hours, *Project Zomboid* and *7 Days to Die* are still the genre kings. Survival Log is the *short-run roguelike* option — the one that respects your time, lets you feel a full arc (plan, panic, survive, die, improve) in a couple of evenings, and costs less than half a Zomboid. It's also the only one of the four where **the pre-disaster shopping list is an actual strategy mechanic**, which is a genuinely fresh angle.

## System Requirements & Platform Notes

- **Minimum:** Windows 10 (64-bit), Intel Core i5-8500 / AMD Ryzen 5 2600X, 16 GB RAM, GeForce GTX 1060 / Radeon RX 6500 XT, DirectX 11, 10 GB storage.
- **Recommended:** Windows 10/11 (64-bit), Intel Core i5-11600K / AMD Ryzen 5 5600, 16 GB RAM, GeForce RTX 3060 / Radeon RX 6700 XT (8 GB+), DirectX 11, 10 GB storage.
- **Windows-only** on Steam — no Mac or Linux builds.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes

### ✅ Do This

- **Follow the tier order on your first run.** Food + water → hardware + melee → radio, fuel, meds. Buying a gun or a TV in hour one is how new players die.
- **Reinforce before you decorate.** Every plank on a window is a zombie that doesn't walk in. The prep phase is short; spend it on survival.
- **Plant a balcony farm on day one of the survival phase.** Renewable calories are the difference between waiting out a horde and starving.
- **Keep the radio on and the journal stocked.** Horde warnings save runs; a journal keeps the mental entropy system from eating your effectiveness.
- **Trade food like currency.** Hoard a surplus, then use it with neighbors and drone trades. Just budget for the neighbor who treats your pantry like their own.
- **Accept death as progression.** You restart with points and a fatter Survival Log. A "failed" run is a stat boost in disguise.
- **Unlock Endless Mode early** if you love the loop — 20 days in Story Mode is a very achievable target once the shopping list clicks.

### ❌ Do Not Do This

- **Don't buy firearms on a first run.** Loud, expensive, and every shot is an invitation. Melee is quieter and works fine.
- **Don't stockpile fresh food.** It rots, and rotten food in a safehouse is wasted money and space.
- **Don't skip the mental side.** Letting anxiety events pile up quietly turns a winnable run into a spiral.
- **Don't over-scavenge early.** Every trip out spends stamina and risks a scratch. If the safehouse can sustain you, grow first, explore second.
- **Don't expect a long sandbox on launch content.** This is a short-run roguelike, not a Zomboid replacement — the marathon is Endless Mode, and the polish notes show in the first week of patches.
- **Don't ignore the language setting if English isn't your thing.** The full Chinese launch coverage is rich, and the game ships Simplified Chinese natively.

## FAQ

### What is Survival Log?
A **zombie-apocalypse hoarding survival sim** from indie developer Midnight Workshop, published by Lilith Games, released **August 12, 2026** on Steam. You die in a zombie outbreak, time-loop back to **10 hours before it starts**, and use that window to hoard supplies, reinforce your home, and survive — with a roguelike loop where every death buys permanent skills.

### Is Survival Log worth it?
At **$9.34 (launch discount)** it's an easy yes if you like *Project Zomboid* or *This War of Mine* but want a shorter, loopier game. It's **Mostly Positive** (73% of 526 reviews) and hit ~6,400 concurrent players in its first days. The main caveats: Windows-only, optimization was rough at launch (patched since), and it's not a hundred-hour sandbox.

### How does the 10-hour countdown work?
The game starts ten hours before the outbreak. Use the time as a priority-shopping-and-fortification budget: **supermarket** for food and water, **hardware store** for reinforcement materials and silent melee, then **fortify home** (board windows, brace doors) and finish with radio, fuel, and medical supplies.

### What should I buy in Survival Log?
Canned and dried food, bottled water and purification tablets, wood planks and steel plates, a hammer and crowbar, a baseball bat or fire axe, a radio, gasoline, and a first-aid kit. **Avoid guns, luxury goods, and fresh produce.**

### How does the rebirth loop work?
When you die, the run restarts but you earn **points** based on your performance, spent on **permanent skills**. Hidden stash locations and survival techniques you discovered are written into your in-game **Survival Log** and carried into future runs.

### How do I unlock Endless Mode in Survival Log?
Survive **20+ days in Story Mode**, and Endless Mode unlocks — a mode for players who want to keep hoarding and fortifying indefinitely.

### Is Survival Log like Project Zomboid?
Spiritually yes — top-down zombie survival with permanent stakes. But it's a **short-run roguelike** with a unique pre-apocalypse prep phase, a mental-health system, and meta-progression, rather than a 100-hour open sandbox. See the comparison table above for the full breakdown.

### What are the system requirements?
Minimum is **Windows 10 64-bit, i5-8500 / Ryzen 5 2600X, 16 GB RAM, GTX 1060 / RX 6500 XT, 10 GB storage**; recommended bumps the CPU to an i5-11600K / Ryzen 5 5600 and the GPU to an RTX 3060 / RX 6700 XT.

### Is Survival Log on Mac or console?
No. It's **Windows-only** on Steam, single-player, with no controller or console versions announced.
