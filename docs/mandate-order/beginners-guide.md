## What Is Mandate Order?

Mandate Order is a city-builder set in China's **Spring and Autumn / Warring States period** (春秋战国). You start as a frontier **"city keeper"** with a single rundown village and slowly build it into a walled state capable of standing among the Seven Powers — **Qi, Chu, Yan, Han, Zhao, Wei, and Qin** (each run randomly brings three of them to your map). It's often compared to *Manor Lords*, and the comparison is fair: both are gridless, logistics-heavy, and make you fight with the same people you feed. But Mandate Order pushes the war further in, fusing real-time tactical battles, siege defense, and a governance layer built on the **Hundred Schools of Thought**.

## The Core Loop: "The City Isn't Decorated — It Runs"

The launch press coverage kept hammering one phrase, and it's the best description of the game: **"The city isn't placed down, it runs"** (城市不是摆出来的，而是运转起来的). Mandate Order's buildings aren't cosmetic collection pieces — they are organs in a living machine. Workers physically haul resources between buildings, roads decide how fast that hauling happens, granaries decide how long you survive a siege, and the state treasury decides whether your retainers stay loyal.

Everything in the loop is connected, and the game never lets you forget it:

- **民夫 (workers)** arrive in squads of five — a **"伍" (wu)** — and each worker needs food, water, housing, and winter fuel.
- **Public opinion (民意)** is a soft cap on your population: happy, fed, housed people reproduce; cold, hungry ones stop arriving.
- **Farming follows the real agrarian calendar** — spring planting, summer weeding, autumn harvest, winter storage.
- **The army is drawn from the same hands that farm and build.** Conscript too aggressively and your fields go to seed mid-harvest; your walls hold, but your granary runs dry.
- **Title progression (爵位)** and diplomacy sit on top: no rank, no foreign policy. The diplomacy button stays locked until you've founded your state.

That last one trips up *everyone* in the first session, so let's get it out of the way: **if diplomacy is greyed out, it's not a bug.** You have to earn it. Here's exactly how.

## How to Unlock Diplomacy: The 爵位 (Title) Progression

New players stare at a locked diplomacy tab for their entire first session and assume it's a placeholder. It isn't — **the foreign-policy screen opens only after you've claimed a title and founded your state.** The steps, which the Bilibili full-playthrough and multiple 3DM guides confirm:

1. **Build the 中军帐 (Central Command Tent).** This is your seat of government — get it up early in your settlement layout.
2. **Meet the promotion conditions** the tent shows you (typically food reserves, population, and prosperity thresholds).
3. **Perform 讨封升爵 ("request a title").** Your lord will grant you your first rank.
4. **Complete 立国 ("found the state").** Your village officially becomes a state on the map.
5. **The diplomacy panel unlocks.** Neighboring states now approach *you* proactively — each run, **three of the seven Warring States** (Qi, Chu, Yan, Han, Zhao, Wei, Qin) appear around your borders.

**My advice from the first session:** as soon as diplomacy opens, find the neighbor who sells whatever you're short of (usually food or timber) and **establish trade before you think about allies**. One reviewer's tactic — "pay tribute early, trade constantly" — is the right read: tribute is cheap early and buys goodwill that pays back in trade deals. Just never forget that **the people who sell you grain are also the people who might siege you an hour later.**

## Your First Hour: The 30-Person Village

The day-one Bilibili showcase puts it perfectly: you start with **a small village and about thirty people**. Everything you do in the first hour is about turning those thirty hands into a stable, growing settlement. Here's the priority order that worked for me:

1. **Housing first, always.** A worker without a home is a worker who leaves — and more importantly, empty housing is what pulls new 民夫 to your land. Build worker housing (庐舍) as soon as you have timber.
2. **Granary and food stores.** Before walls, before temples, before anything pretty — food is the only thing that keeps your population from stalling. Get a granary up and fill it.
3. **Farmland on your best soil.** Mandate Order uses a real seasonal cycle, so timing matters: plant in spring, tend in summer, harvest in autumn, store for winter. Miss the window and you eat through reserves all winter.
4. **Water and fuel.** Wells/water access and winter fuel are easy to forget until the first cold season hits and population growth halts.
5. **The Central Command Tent.** Now that you're stable, start the 爵位 process (above) so diplomacy unlocks before you *need* it.
6. **A wall, however short.** Your first defensive perimeter doesn't need to be grand — a simple wall ring with a gate buys you time when the first raid comes. The game makes walls genuinely matter (see below), so get something on the ground early.
7. **Don't conscript yet.** With thirty people, every soldier is a missing farmer. Hold off until your first surplus is banked in the granary.

**The single most common beginner mistake:** trying to do all of this at once and running out of food mid-winter. Mandate Order punishes over-ambition with cold, hungry workers who stop reproducing — and a city that isn't growing is a city that's dying.

## The 伍 System: Workers, Needs & Public Opinion

The labor system is the backbone of the whole game, and it's unusually elegant. Workers (**民夫**) don't trickle in one by one — they arrive in **squads of five called "伍" (wu)**, the ancient Chinese military-and-civilian squad unit. Here's what the guides all agree on:

- **One 伍 is rarely enough for a building.** A single squad is inefficient; most production buildings want **at least two 伍** assigned to run at a reasonable pace. Staff thin at your own risk.
- **Every worker needs four things:** food, water, housing, and winter fuel. Hit all four and your population grows; miss any and growth stalls or reverses.
- **Public opinion (民意) is your population thermostat.** Low opinion caps immigration and reproduction even when food is abundant. Temple/ritual buildings and festivals (国家祭祀) raise it.
- **Workers are physical.** They walk, haul, and queue. A long road between the farm and the granary isn't a cosmetic choice — it's lost labor hours. **Plan logistics before you build.**

**The logistics lesson:** the game has distinct building roles — **granaries** (food), **fuel depots** (winter wood), **armories** (weapons for conscripts), **warehouses** (general goods), and **courier stations** (movement speed). Getting the *placement* of these right matters as much as building them at all. I rebuild my courier station network twice in the first week before it clicked: **short, direct roads between producers and storers is the whole game.**

## Seasonal Farming & the Rural Economy

Mandate Order doesn't do the "plant anytime, harvest in four days" farming sim thing. It runs a **real four-season agrarian cycle**:

| Season | Work |
|:-------|:-----|
| 🌱 **Spring** | Plow and plant — get your seasonal crops in early |
| ☀️ **Summer** | Weed and tend — a missed summer reduces yield |
| 🌾 **Autumn** | Harvest — the big grain income of the year |
| ❄️ **Winter** | Store and ration — eat down what you harvested, burn fuel |

Crops, weather, and **natural disasters and disease** all factor in (the developer has tuned disease and disaster systems across the EA period). The strategy is to **oversize your autumn harvest** so winter storage covers you until the next spring — because a bad harvest year with a full granary is a bad year; a bad harvest year with an empty granary is a population collapse.

**The trade decision that defines the game:** grain feeds people *and* funds the state *and* (later) feeds armies. Every bushel has three competing uses, and there's no "right" answer — only what your current situation demands.

## The Six Schools of Thought (百家): Which Retainer to Hire

Mandate Order's governance layer is the feature that makes it stand out from every western city-builder. You recruit **retainers (门客)** from six philosophical schools — the famous **Hundred Schools of Thought** compressed to the six that matter for gameplay. Retainers sit in government, military, and diplomacy slots, and each school bends the game in a different direction.

![The six schools of thought in Mandate Order — Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, Legalist, Military and Agricultural retainers](/assets/images/mandate-order/mandate-order-schools.webp)

| School | Playstyle | What its retainers/policies do |
|:-------|:----------|:-------------------------------|
| 🏮 **Confucian (儒家)** | **Best for beginners** | The only school that **actively supplies workers** — build empty housing and it fills monthly. Cheap retainers, low wages, production boosts that stack across recipes. Policy: extra sacrifice income, +50% foreign-garrison capacity, 30% chance soldiers block ranged shots |
| ☯️ **Daoist (道家)** | Gathering specialist | Retainers **replenish resources at gathering points** instead of depleting them — the resource node doesn't shrink while they work |
| 🛠️ **Mohist (墨家)** | **Machine swarm** | Gatherers bring **extra goods per haul**; turn raw logs into **machine parts (wheels, bearings, frames)** at huge efficiency for ballistae/catapults, or sell them; retainers repair buildings and siege engines. Policy: sacrifices refund materials, wheeled machines knock enemies back, global self-repair |
| ⚖️ **Legalist (法家)** | High-skill, resource-hungry | Complex playstyle heavily **dependent on copper mines** and pairing with the Yin-Yang school — powerful when you understand it, punishing when you don't |
| ⚔️ **Military (兵家)** | War support | Retainers in an army boost **move speed, range, and attack**; named scholars can **double archer range**. Policy: faster ranged fire (also applies to ballistae/catapults), more demolition damage, melee can trip enemies. Its unique kitchen recipe (**wood → rice porridge**) creates a renewable food line that bypasses farms |
| 🌾 **Agricultural (农家)** | **Reliable growth** | Fast planting and multi-harvest in the fields; work produces **water, fertilizer, and knife coins**. Combat retainers **heal every living soldier** in their squad — with full armor, effectively unstoppable. Policy: global boosts, halved retainer needs, kitchen generates money |

**My school recommendation for a first run: Confucian (儒家).** It's the community's nickname "the wheelchair" for a reason — it removes the population bottleneck, which is the thing that kills most first runs. Once you understand the loop, branch into **Mohist** for the siege-engine economy or **Agricultural** for a farming-first state. Military is a great *second* school; on its own it's weak in the early game when there's nothing to fight with yet.

## Walls, Armies & Siege Warfare

This is where Mandate Order earns its "war" label. Unlike most city-builders where defenses are an afterthought, **your city wall design directly determines siege outcomes** — the launch press release literally framed walls, roads, and armories as "a test of your entire settlement."

**The army you field is your workforce.** There's no separate soldier class:

- Conscript **民夫** into **infantry (步兵), archers (弓兵), and chariots (战车)**.
- **Weapons have to be produced and stored first** — an armory full of bows and swords is what turns a farmer into a soldier. Build the production chain *before* you raise the levy.
- Battles run in **real time with line-drawing formation commands** — you draw the line, your troops take formation. Battles can involve **thousands of soldiers** on screen.

**Walls are free-form and functional:**

- Place walls, gates, and towers wherever you want — **elevation affects combat**, so high-ground walls are worth the extra stone.
- **Archers garrison walls** and fire down on siegers.
- **Crossbow turrets (床弩) and catapults (投石车)** are the heavy hitters — and this is where the **Military school's "faster ranged fire" policy shines**, because it boosts siege engines too.
- The **Mohist school** enables an "engine swarm" (器械海) strategy: mass-produce machine parts and outgun siege attacks.

**The balance trap to avoid:** every soldier is a farmer who isn't farming. Over-conscript and your granary empties; under-conscript and the walls fall. The game's whole tension lives in this slider, and it's why the launch materials describe Mandate Order as "not a game where you build first and fight later."

## Mandate Order vs Manor Lords: A Data Comparison

Every review compares Mandate Order to *Manor Lords*, and it's the right comparison to make. Here's the data:

| Dimension | Mandate Order | Manor Lords |
|:----------|:--------------|:------------|
| **Setting** | Spring & Autumn China (春秋战国) | Medieval Europe |
| **Price** | $19.99 (¥68) | $39.99 |
| **Early Access** | Aug 12, 2026 | Apr 26, 2024 |
| **Engine** | Unreal Engine 5 | Unreal Engine 4 |
| **Workers** | 伍 squads of 5 | Individual families |
| **Philosophy layer** | Six schools of thought + retainers | None |
| **Army source** | Conscripted from the same workforce | Militia mustered from families |
| **Combat** | Real-time line-drawing, thousands of soldiers | Real-time unit squads, smaller scale |
| **Seasons** | Four-season agrarian calendar | Four seasons, winter fuel |
| **Diplomacy** | Gated behind rank (爵位) | Barter & regional power, no formal diplomacy |
| **Roadmap** | 12-month EA → 1.0 | Long EA, still in 0.8+ |

**The honest verdict from my playtime:** if *Manor Lords* is a medieval sandbox where war is one pillar among many, Mandate Order is a *strategy game that happens to be a city-builder*. The philosophy system, the rank-gated diplomacy, and the "your workers are your army" tension make it think more like *Total War* or *Age of Empires* governance than a pure builder. Manor Lords is the friendlier entry point; Mandate Order is the deeper strategic puzzle — and at $19.99 it's priced to be the "try both" option.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes

### ✅ Do This

- **Build empty worker housing early.** Empty homes are what pull new 伍 to your land — it's the cheapest population growth in the game.
- **Over-build your autumn harvest.** Winter eats reserves; a big granary is the difference between a bumpy year and a collapse.
- **Unlock diplomacy fast.** Get the Central Command Tent up, claim your title, found your state — trade income changes the whole early game.
- **Plan logistics before buildings.** Short, direct roads between farm and granary, granary and kitchen, armory and wall. Couriers only fix bad layouts they can't save.
- **Staff buildings with at least two 伍.** One squad runs everything at a crawl.
- **Pick Confucian for your first run.** The population safety net is worth more than any other school's combat bonus early.
- **Produce weapons before you conscript.** An army without an armory is just farmers with opinions.

### ❌ Do Not Do This

- **Do not conscript in the first hour.** With ~30 workers, every soldier is a lost farmer.
- **Do not treat walls as decoration.** In Mandate Order, wall placement, gate position, and armory location decide siege outcomes.
- **Do not ignore public opinion.** Low 民意 caps your population no matter how full the granary is.
- **Do not buy seeds/crops for the wrong season.** The agrarian calendar is real; planting at the wrong time is a full-year waste.
- **Do not expect the diplomacy tab to work before you've earned a title.** It's not a bug — go found your state.
- **Do not buy "out of support."** The developer says so themselves; buy if the EA content fits you.

## FAQ

### What is Mandate Order (烽沙)?
An Eastern war-themed city-builder from Chengdu studio **Digital Sky**, set in China's Spring and Autumn / Warring States period. You grow a 30-person border village into a walled, diplomatically active state — farming, governing with six schools of thought, and fighting real-time battles with soldiers conscripted from your own workforce. It launched into **Early Access on August 12, 2026** for **$19.99 (¥68)**.

### Is Mandate Order worth it in Early Access?
If you like *Manor Lords*, *Banished*, or *Total War*-style governance, yes — the core loop is complete and genuinely deep, and at $19.99 with a 15% launch discount it's priced to be easy to try. If you need polished UI and full content, the ~12-month EA window means waiting is a reasonable choice.

### How do I unlock diplomacy in Mandate Order?
Build the **Central Command Tent (中军帐)**, meet its promotion conditions, request a title (讨封升爵), found your state (立国), and the diplomacy panel opens. Three of the seven Warring States will then appear around your borders.

### What is the best school of thought for beginners?
**Confucian (儒家)**. It's the only school that actively provides workers, which removes the population bottleneck that kills most first runs. Military (兵家) works best as a second school.

### How does the 伍 worker system work?
Workers arrive in squads of five called **伍 (wu)**. Each worker needs food, water, housing, and winter fuel. Most buildings need at least two 伍 to run efficiently, and low public opinion (民意) caps population growth.

### Are walls actually useful?
Yes — more than in almost any other city-builder. Wall placement, gate position, towers, and armory locations directly determine siege outcomes, and archers garrison walls while ballistae and catapults (boosted by the Military school) do the heavy damage.

### What's the difference between Mandate Order and Manor Lords?
Setting (Warring States China vs medieval Europe), price ($19.99 vs $39.99), and depth: Mandate Order adds a six-school philosophy governance layer, rank-gated diplomacy, and a tighter "your workers are your army" tension. Manor Lords is friendlier; Mandate Order is deeper and cheaper.

### What's coming in the Early Access roadmap?
New map and minimap in August, Level-3 building upgrades in September, animal husbandry (chickens, dogs, cattle) in October, and the "Cave Heaven" system, Panda Warriors, and new defense gear in November–December, with a Battle of Changping story campaign later.
