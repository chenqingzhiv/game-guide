## What Is Manor Lords in 2026?

Manor Lords is a medieval settlement-builder where every worker is a *person*, not an abstract pop number. Your **families** live in burgage plots, walk to their workplaces every morning, carry tools, and eat what your village actually produces. It layers a surprisingly deep logistics and production-chain simulation on top of large-scale tactical battles — think *Banished* or *Farthest Frontier* crossed with *Total War*-style combat.

The game is still in Early Access at **version 0.8**, and it has changed enormously since launch. Here's what matters right now:

## Step 1: Set Up a Beginner-Friendly Game

Your first few runs should be about learning the economy, not fighting for survival. Here's the setup I recommend:

| Setting | Recommendation | Why |
|:--------|:--------------|:----|
| **Scenario** | "Rise to Prosperity" | No bandit raids, no harsh weather — pure city-builder sandbox |
| **Difficulty** | Relaxed | Family approval is forgiving; workers are faster |
| **Map** | Any rich lowland region | Plenty of fertile soil + berry deposits + deer to learn on |
| **Speed** | Pause (Spacebar) | You can plan your whole village layout before the clock starts |

**Pro tip:** Unpause the game only when you know exactly what your first five buildings are going to be. A paused plan is worth 30 minutes of rework.

## Step 2: The First-Hour Build Order

The single biggest beginner mistake is building too much, too fast. Your starting workforce is tiny (usually **3–4 families** plus a few oxen), and every building you place needs a worker assigned. Over-build, and your villagers spend all day walking and never producing.

Here's the build order I follow every single run:

| # | Building | What it does | Why first |
|:-:|:---------|:-------------|:----------|
| 1 | **Logging Camp** | Chopping camp producing **timber** | Timber is the backbone of everything you build |
| 2 | **Woodcutter's Lodge** | Produces **firewood** | Firewood keeps families warm — skip this and winter kills you |
| 3 | **Storehouse** | Stores raw materials (logs, stone, hides) | Stops your goods rotting in the open |
| 4 | **Granary** | Stores food | Same reason — food spoilage is a real mechanic since Update 3 |
| 5 | **Burgage Plots** (2–3) | Houses for your families | More homes → more families → more workers |
| 6 | **Forager Hut** | Collects **berries** | A renewable food source from day one |
| 7 | **Hunting Camp** | Produces **meat + hides** | Meat fills your second food slot |
| 8 | **Marketplace** | Distributes food & fuel to homes | Families stop eating raw berries if a stall is nearby |

**The #1 rule:** Always leave **at least one family unassigned**. Unassigned families handle construction and ox-driving. Assign everyone and construction stalls completely.

## Step 3: Food & Firewood — the Real Survival Bar

Manor Lords has no "you're starving" popup in the same way other games do — the consequences are slower and sneakier. If families go hungry or cold, **approval drops**, and when approval drops, **families stop arriving**. The game quietly stalls.

**Your food goals, in order:**

| Food source | Buildings | Notes |
|:------------|:----------|:------|
| 🫐 **Berries** | Forager Hut | Set "cap" so workers don't strip the deposit bare — it regrows over time |
| 🦌 **Meat** | Hunting Camp | Place it near deer trails; **don't overhunt** a single herd |
| 🥚 **Eggs** | Chicken Coop (burgage backyard) | Year-round food, zero field space |
| 🥕 **Vegetables** | Vegetable Garden (burgage backyard) | Big harvest, but slow to mature — plant in spring |
| 🌾 **Bread** | Farm → Windmill → Bakery | The "real" food chain, later |

Aim for **two distinct food types by your first winter**, and **three by your second year**. Each extra food type gives a big approval bonus.

**Firewood is a year-round job.** In winter, families burn roughly **double the firewood** — I've watched villages collapse in Month 2 of winter purely from fuel running out. Build a **second Woodcutter's Lodge before the first frost** if your stockpile is under ~50.

## Step 4: Burgage Plots, Families & Approval

Your burgage plots are the heart of the whole game. Each occupied plot hosts one family, and families are your entire labor force. The loop is:

**Build plot → family moves in → approval rises → new family arrives → build another plot → repeat.**

**What raises approval:**
- Plenty of food (variety matters more than quantity)
- Warm homes (firewood supply)
- Market access (build a marketplace with food stalls)
- A church with spare capacity (needed for Level 2 upgrades)

**What tanks approval:**
- Cold homes (no firewood in winter)
- Single-food diets
- Taxing families too early (wait until Level 2+)
- Unserved families (no market stall nearby)

Since the **0.8.059 update, settlement level progression checks your family count** rather than built plot levels. That means the road to bigger settlements is: keep those plots **occupied and upgraded**, not sprawling empty shells.

> 💡 **Backyard trick:** When you upgrade a burgage plot, you can add a backyard extension. **Chicken Coops are the best early value** — eggs year-round with almost no micromanagement.

## Step 5: Development Points — What to Unlock First

You earn **Development Points** every time your settlement levels up. Click your settlement's name at the top of the UI to open the tree. These are my top early picks, in order:

| Development | Branch | Why |
|:------------|:-------|:----|
| **Forest Management** | Farming | **Doubles berry yield** from deposits — early food surplus you can also export |
| **Charcoal Burning** | Industry | Charcoal Kiln turns **1 firewood → 2 charcoal** — effectively doubles your fuel |
| **Trapping** | Farming | Extra food from hunting — another food variety source |
| **Trade Logistics** | Trade | Cuts trade route costs — start importing tools/iron sooner |
| **Heavy Plow** | Farming | Big efficiency boost once you move to real farming in year 2 |

**Avoid** locking into specialized crafts (armor smithing, deep mining, etc.) until you've seen your region's resources. The early game is about **food + fuel + families**, not the endgame.

## Step 6: Survive Your First Winter

Winter is where the tutorial ends and the actual game begins. Here's the checklist I run through every year before the first snowfall:

- [ ] **Firewood stockpile of 50+** (ideally 100+ by year 2)
- [ ] **Two food types** stored in the Granary
- [ ] **A second woodcutter** running full-time
- [ ] **Burgage plots upgraded** with chicken coops
- [ ] **Hunting/foraging camps** with caps set so they don't over-harvest
- [ ] **Charcoal Kiln built** if you took the development (double fuel)

**A very common winter trap:** your farming families go idle in winter (fields can't be worked). That's *expected* — reassign them to woodcutting, or use the time to build. Idle hands are a resource in Manor Lords, not a problem.

## Step 7: Farms, Crafts & Trade (Year 2+)

Once your village is stable, the economy opens up. The three "real" production chains worth building first:

| Chain | Field crop → Processing | Output |
|:------|:------------------------|:-------|
| **Bread** | Wheat → Windmill → Bakery | The best bulk food source |
| **Clothes** | Flax → Linen → Weaving | Warmth for winter + export goods |
| **Ale** | Barley → Malt → Tavern | Approval booster once your plots hit Level 2+ |

**Trade is your growth lever.** Build a **Trading Post**, attach a horse or ox, and export what your region makes too much of (planks, wool, hides, linen) to import what it lacks (tools, iron, stone). Trade routes cost influence — another reason **Trade Logistics** is a top early pick.

> 💡 **Don't build a bakery before a windmill, and don't build a windmill before a wheat field.** Sounds obvious, but the game happily lets you build a whole food-processing chain with nothing feeding it. Check the production tab *before* placing.

## Step 8: Defense — Militia, Manor & the First Raid

Even in non-"Rise to Prosperity" games, bandits start appearing once your settlement grows. Here's the defense ladder:

1. **Burgage plots are your army.** Every adult male can be mustered into militia — equip them with whatever you can craft (spears, bows, axes).
2. **Build a Manor** to unlock taxation, regional policies, and **towers**. A single well-placed tower deters early bandit groups.
3. **Use the terrain.** A narrow bridge choke-point beats an open field every time. The 0.8 pathfinding update also means squads now fan out sensibly in open ground and file into columns on roads.

**Raid math since the raider rework:** bandits loot until they've taken ~half your valuables or torched ~40% of your buildings, then they leave. That means a **partial defense — just stalling them — can save your village**. You don't always need to wipe the band out.

## The Beginner Mistakes I Made So You Don't

1. **Built 10 buildings on day one.** Five focused buildings beat ten half-staffed ones.
2. **Assigned every single family.** No construction workers → nothing ever gets built.
3. **Ignored firewood in fall.** Winter fuel doubled and my first village froze in Month 2 of winter.
4. **Farmed in year one.** Farming is labor-heavy and slow — hunt and forage first.
5. **Stripped the berry deposit.** Setting the "cap" at ~60% keeps berries regrowing forever.
6. **Taxed at Level 1.** Approval is fragile early; wait until your plots are upgraded.
7. **Built the bakery first.** Check the input chain before placing processors.
8. **Skipped the Marketplace.** Families who can't reach a stall go "unserved" and approval craters.

## Manor Lords Beginner Checklist — TL;DR

1. **Setup:** "Rise to Prosperity" on Relaxed, pick a fertile map, pause and plan.
2. **Hour one:** Logging Camp → Woodcutter's Lodge → Storehouse → Granary → Burgage Plots → Forager Hut → Hunting Camp → Marketplace.
3. **Keep one family unassigned** for construction, always.
4. **Two food types by winter, three by year 2** — berries, meat, then eggs.
5. **Firewood stockpile 50+ before frost** — winter fuel use doubles.
6. **Unlock Forest Management + Charcoal Burning first** in Development.
7. **Upgrade plots with chicken coops** and keep families housed to level up.
8. **Build a Manor early** to unlock towers and the tax you'll need later.
9. **Partial defense counts** — stalling raiders until they leave is a win.

Manor Lords is at its most rewarding once the survival basics click and you can stop firefighting and start *designing*. Get the first winter right and the whole game opens up. Good luck, my lord.
