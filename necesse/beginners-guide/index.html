    🌍 Necesse Beginner's Guide 2026 — First Day, Settlements & Boss Progression

    Developer: Fair Games ApS | Publisher: Fair Games ApS
    Steam Price: $14.99 (often 50% off) | Current Version: 1.3.0 "Rust & Romance"
    Genre: Action-RPG / colony sim / sandbox survival
    Platforms: PC (Steam), Steam Deck Verified

    I've sunk more than 80 hours into Necesse across two worlds and the 1.3.0 free weekend, and it's the most underrated sandbox-survival game on Steam right now — 93% positive from 27,600+ reviews, and it deserves every one. This guide takes you from your very first chopping swing to your first fully automated, raid-proof settlement, with the exact priorities, data, and boss order I wish I'd had.

    What Is Necesse, Really?

    Necesse is Terraria's mining and crafting crossed with RimWorld's settlement automation, in an infinitely generated world. You fight dungeon-crawler-style bosses, but the game's signature is your settlement: villagers who farm, chop, craft, haul, and defend on their own. The 1.3.0 update added settler personalities, recreation, and romance quests — your colony now feels alive instead of like a conveyor belt.

    The core loop: Survive the night → expand your base → attract settlers → automate everything → beat bosses → unlock deeper caves → repeat.

    First Day: Survival Basics

    Your first in-game day is a race against nightfall. The night spawns enemies that will kill a naked new player, so the rule is simple: before dark, have a tool, a weapon, and a shelter.

    Step-by-step first-day checklist

    Priority Action Why it matters
    1️⃣ Chop 50–100 wood, mine some stone Wood and stone are the bedrock of every tool and structure
    2️⃣ Craft a Workbench (E inventory → place with right-click) Unlocks the starter tool and weapon recipes
    3️⃣ Craft Wooden Axe, Wooden Pickaxe, Wooden Sword The pickaxe and axe are make-or-break for gathering; the sword is your survival line
    4️⃣ Find food — hunt rabbits/chickens/sheep, grab berries Starvation drains health; cooked meat restores far more than raw
    5️⃣ Build a Campfire (wood + stone) Cooks meat; warmth near early spawn zones
    6️⃣ Build a shelter before night — walls, a door, a torch, a bed Sleep skips the night and sets your respawn point
    7️⃣ Fight cautiously — goblins and slimes only Learn their attack patterns; dodge, don't trade hits

    Pro tip: Fallen logs and scattered rocks give you an instant start without walking far. Keep a spare wooden pickaxe — the first thing that breaks will be the tool you need most.

    First Base: What to Build

    You don't need a castle on day one. You need four things:

    1. Workbench — the primary crafting station for gear, structures, and the tools that keep you alive.
    2. Furnace — smelts ore into ingots and cooks food. Place it as soon as you find your first copper.
    3. Storage Chests — organize your inventory and, critically, let settlers access materials later.
    4. Leather armor — craft from animal hides before you push past the starting biome.

    Where to build

    Pick a spot that is flat, close to wood + stone + water, and naturally defensible — a clearing ringed by trees is ideal. Avoid areas with constant enemy spawns and leave room to expand: your settlement will grow faster than you expect.

    Settlements: Housing, Jobs & Automation

    This is where Necesse stops being a survival game and becomes something special. Once you place a Settlement Flag, NPCs start wandering in — and specialized settlers will automate almost everything you'd otherwise do by hand.

    Housing requirements

    Every settler needs a valid house to stay happy and join your colony:

    Requirement Detail
    Enclosed room Four walls + a door (fully enclosed)
    Bed Mandatory — no bed, no move-in
    Light source A torch, lantern, or candelabra
    Flooring Any material counts
    Size At least ~5×5 inside; larger rooms give a happiness bonus (50+ tiles = +35)
    Homeless cap Up to 2 homeless settlers; more need an empty room with a bed

    Specialized settlers have extra requirements — an Alchemist needs an Alchemist Table, a Blacksmith needs an Anvil. The game tells you exactly what a settler needs when you try to move them in.

    Specialized settlers worth seeking out

    Settler What they automate
    Blacksmith Automated gear repairs and high-tier crafting
    Alchemist Automatic potion brewing
    Farmer Plants, harvests, and replants crops in farming zones
    Forester / Lumberjack Chops trees in forestry zones
    Hunter / Angler Passive food income
    Pawnbroker Sells your excess loot for gold without leaving base
    Guard Defends the settlement during raids

    Recruiting: NPCs with question marks over their heads wander to your flag. You can also find them in other villages (Forest, Snow, Plains, Desert biomes), dungeons, enemy bases, and campsites. Some want payment — a Hunter may join for 240 coin or 34 leather. Mages can be freed from dungeon prisons.

    Work zoning and priorities

    • Press C for the Settlement Interface — assign work priorities (1–5) for hauling, farming, chopping, and mining.
    • Press Z for the zoning menu:
    • Farming Zone — settlers automatically plant, harvest, and replant near tilled soil. Keep a chest of seeds nearby.
    • Forestry Zone — settlers chop mature trees and replant saplings.
    • Husbandry Zone — with a fenced area, gate, and Feeding Trough (wheat), lead wild sheep/cows in with a rope and they'll breed; set a max count and excess get slaughtered.
    • Fertilize Zone — settlers apply fertilizer to speed crop growth.
    • Chest filters — right-click a chest, enable the settlement button, and restrict allowed items. Haulers auto-sort loot into the right chests.

    Keeping settlers happy and fast

    • Food variety — gourmet food gives +35% happiness; 12+ food varieties gives +40%; a Satiated buff from quality food raises work efficiency up to +25%.
    • Furniture — 7 unique furniture types (Bed, Table, Chair, Dresser, Clock, Flower Pot, Candelabra) max out furniture happiness at +20%.
    • Avoid barracks — shared bedrooms hurt hard: two settlers in one room is −20 happiness, five is −50.
    • Use coolers — cooler boxes (fueled by Ice Blossoms) stop food spoilage.

    The Full Boss Progression Order

    The Elder NPC's questline is your intended path. Each quest points you at a boss, and completing them raises raid difficulty — so pace yourself. This is the order that works:

    # Boss Where / How Key Loot Gear Tier
    1 Evil's Protector Summon with a Mysterious Idol at night Demonic Bars, Demon Heart (permanent max HP) Copper / Tin
    2 Void Wizard Void Cave Void Mask, Deep Cave Key materials Demonite
    3 Swamp Guardian Deep Swamp Ivy (poison-resistant gear) Demonite+
    4 Ancient Vulture Deep Desert pyramid ruins Quartz, Vulture Staff (summoner) Quartz
    5 Pirate Captain ⭐ Cursed Compass on a Pirate Island Deep Cave Ladder — unlocks deep underground Tungsten
    6 Reaper Deep Forest graveyard at night Reaper Scythe Shadow
    7 Sage and Grit (Twin Dragons) Desert Dragon Idol in the Deep Desert Glacier fragments Glacier
    8 Fallen Wizard Lava Castle Unlocks Incursions (endgame) Obsidian

    ⭐ Pirate Captain is the milestone. His Deep Cave Ladder permanently unlocks the deep underground layers (Tungsten ore), splitting the game into early/mid/late. If you only remember one boss, remember this one.

    Boss fight notes

    • Evil's Protector — the classic night-time summon. Bring the best Copper/Tin gear you can craft and clear nearby adds first.
    • Sage and Grit — the twin dragons must die near-simultaneously. If one dies while the other is above ~20% HP, the survivor enrages (300% damage, health regen). Rotate targets to keep their health bars even.
    • Fallen Wizard — multi-phase bullet-hell. Phase 2 turns the floor to lava: bring Flame Resistance Potions and the Hovering Tool.
    • Re-fighting bosses gives guaranteed unique drops (round-robin), and raid drops can include high-tier gear — a great source of equipment.

    Gear tier order

    Spider → Frost → Demonite/Demonic → Ivy → Quartz → Tungsten → Shadow → Glacier → Obsidian

    Don't treat every tier as a must-craft: gear is often a sidegrade. Demonic armor (Demonic Bars + Void Shards) stays viable well into the late game thanks to its high defense.

    Automated Base Defense — Stop Raids Cold

    Raids scale as you complete Elder quests and your settlement grows wealthy. The best defense isn't more walls — it's traps wired into a kill zone. The full setup is in our Rust & Romance update coverage, but here are the essentials:

    1. Traps can't be crafted — they're world drops. Arrow and flame traps come from caves; Void traps and Glyph traps (Bounce, Chicken, Reverse Damage) from the Dungeon. Trap quality scales with cave tier (Stone → Deep Stone → Sandstone → Swamp Stone → Snow Stone → Granite → Ice → Obsidian → Basalt).
    2. Funnel raiders — they prefer open terrain > doors > walls. Build a thick perimeter (2–6 tiles) with one or two controlled entrances, zig-zag hallways with doors, and lay mud tiles in the approach to slow them.
    3. Wire the traps — traps sit dormant until connected. Use sensors (fire only when hostiles are ~5 squares away) and timers set to 22 ticks — a 20-tick timer makes traps skip a cycle.
    4. Don't trap your own villagers — use an inverted zone over the kill area, or an AND gate wired to a lever you flip during raids.
    5. Keep guards one tier below your gear (never two), aim for 13–16 guards, and give them area-of-effect or piercing weapons — the Void Staff is a standout early-to-mid.

    10 Things I Wish I Knew

    1. Prioritize wood + stone before anything else — 100 wood on day one beats a fancy sword.
    2. The Elder's quests are the map — follow them for progression, but don't rush; each boss raises raid difficulty.
    3. Hire specialized settlers, not plain villagers — Blacksmith, Alchemist, Farmer, and Pawnbroker each remove a whole chore.
    4. A room is not a home without a bed — beds are mandatory, and free beds get claimed automatically.
    5. Use chest filters early — your haulers are only as smart as your chest settings.
    6. Traps are world drops, not recipes — mine every cave you find and hoard the good ones.
    7. Keep bosses balanced — the Twin Dragons enrage if killed apart; rotate targets.
    8. Pirate Captain is the real midpoint — his Deep Cave Ladder changes everything.
    9. Food variety beats food quantity — 12+ varieties and the Satiated buff make settlers dramatically faster.
    10. Old saves work after 1.3.0, but start fresh — the reworked intro and bigger biomes are worth the restart.

    FAQ

    Q: Is Necesse single-player friendly? Yes — the entire game is fully playable solo, and co-op multiplayer is supported. Boss HP scales with player count.

    Q: How long is a playthrough? A full run to the endgame Incursions is roughly 40–80 hours. A focused beginner playthrough through the Pirate Captain milestone is more like 15–25 hours.

    Q: Does the 1.3.0 update break my save? Old saves are compatible, but the developers recommend a fresh world to fully experience the bigger biomes and reworked intro.

    Q: What's the best beginner weapon? The Vulture Staff (summoner) stays strong through the mid-game, and the Thunder Hammer hits through walls. For pure defense, the Void Staff is the standout early-to-mid pick.

    Q: Is it worth it at $7.49 on sale? At its frequent 50% discount it's a no-brainer — 93% positive, Steam Deck verified, and 40+ hours of content for less than a pizza.
