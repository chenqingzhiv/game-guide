    🪐 StarRupture Beginner's Guide 2026 — Survive & Automate Arcadia-7

    Developer: Creepy Jar | Publisher: Creepy Jar Steam Price: $19.99 (launched at $15.99 for the first week) | Status: Early Access since Jan 6, 2026 Genre: Open-world survival / factory automation / base building Platforms: PC (Steam) | Multiplayer: Solo or up to 4-player co-op

    StarRupture is the breakout factory-survival game of 2026: Creepy Jar — the Polish studio behind Green Hell — drops you on Arcadia-7, a hostile alien planet whose star erupts in repeated cataclysms. It's Satisfactory meets Factorio on an alien planet, with survival mechanics and alien hordes on top. It topped Steam's best-seller charts within days of its Early Access launch, and its Mostly Positive rating says the factory loop is the real deal. This guide gets you through the first hour, the first Rupture, and your first fully automated production line.

    What Is StarRupture, Really?

    You are a prisoner serving a sentence on Arcadia-7, doing the only work that matters out here: mining, refining, and shipping resources for the five corporations that run the planet. The catch — beyond your sentence — is the environment. The planet's sun, Ruptura, periodically erupts in solar storms that incinerate the surface. Between eruptions you build, automate, and expand; when the warnings sound, you drop everything and get inside.

    The core loop: Hand-mine resources → build automation (excavators, rails, smelters) → refine ores into components → ship goods to orbital corporations via the Orbital Cargo Launcher → unlock better tech → survive the next Rupture and build bigger.

    If you like: Satisfactory, Factorio, Subnautica, Valheim — StarRupture sits at the junction of factory automation and hostile-planet survival. For more automation, see the Satisfactory guide hub; for more hostile-planet survival, see the Subnautica guide hub.

    Your Vital Signs: Calories, Hydration & Toxicity

    Your HUD tracks five things that actually matter for survival. The first two are the classic hunger/thirst pair, the third is the survival-game twist that makes Arcadia-7 special, and the last two cover your combat readiness and stamina.

    Stat What it does How to fix it
    🔥 Calories Hunger — you need a steady supply Eat Polifruit (orange plants) and cooked Food Station meals
    💧 Hydration Thirst — drains fast while running Drink from Hydrobulbs (blue flowers near water) and purified sources
    ☣️ Toxicity Contamination — if it hits 100 you vomit and lose most calories & hydration Avoid toxic zones, use Antidote Gel, don't eat contaminated plants
    🛡️ Shield / Health Shield regenerates after a few seconds out of combat; Health only returns via food Cooked meals restore Health; carry healing items
    ⚡ Energy Stamina for running and double-jumping Recharges on its own; don't sprint constantly

    Early-game food plan: snack on Polifruit for calories and Hydrobulbs for hydration — both restore their stat with no toxicity penalty, which is more than you can say for most of the local flora. As soon as you can, build a Food Station (Future Health Solutions) so meals start covering health, calories, and hydration at once.

    The Rupture Cycle: The Event That Defines the Game

    Ruptura's eruptions aren't random — they're a cycle, and the game tells you before they hit. Here's the exact sequence you'll learn to live with:

    1. "Wave Warning" appears — twice.
    2. A 15-second countdown starts.
    3. Ruptura erupts. You get roughly 5 extra seconds of grace before the surface becomes instantly lethal.
    4. Being inside any building keeps you safe. Outdoors = death, no exceptions.

    Make the Rupture work for you. Eruptions are destructive but profitable:

    • Surface plants temporarily die, but rare materials spawn — most importantly Ignitium, the fiery orange rocks that convert to huge amounts of Data Points.
    • Underground caves grow Glowcap mushrooms after a Rupture — ~90 Data Points each.
    • Enemies respawn and get more aggressive, so treat post-Rupture scavenging runs as combat trips: bring ammo.

    The cardinal rule for exploring: always carry an emergency kit — 1 Meteor Heart (for a base core) and 100+ Basic Building Materials — so you can throw down an emergency Habitat and airlock when the countdown starts. Everything deconstructs for a 100% material refund, so the kit costs you nothing in the long run.

    Your First Hour: Exact Priorities

    Don't wander. Here's the order that gets you from "stranded convict" to "owns a working factory" fastest.

    1. Drop your Base Core early. It expands your build range. You can place more cores later to claim outposts.
    2. Stabilize power. Your first Solar Generator v.1 is the difference between a base that works and a base that doesn't. Nothing runs without juice.
    3. Hand-mine Titanium and Wolfram — the two ores around your landing zone — until you clear Training Level 2 and unlock the Ore Excavator.
    4. Put one automated loop online: Ore Excavator → Rail v.1 → short storage handoff. Keep it simple; the point is that it runs.
    5. Push Training toward the Smelter (around Level 4). Build one clean line: Excavator → Rail → Smelter → storage.
    6. Unlock the Fabricator via Selenian Level 2. This is the game's first big milestone — the Fabricator turns bars into the components every mid-game recipe needs.

    Mining mechanics worth knowing: your mining tool has a heat bar — press the reload key (R) just before it caps to vent heat instead of waiting for an overheat cooldown. Fully mining a deposit fills a combo bar that makes mining faster, and aiming at the inclusions (the brighter chunks inside a boulder) ramps the combo fastest.

    Ores & Resources: What to Mine, and Where

    Arcadia-7 currently has six things worth mining (one of them appears only after Ruptures):

    Ore Where to find it What it's for
    Wolfram Most common — copper-brown arch veins near the lander and to the north Early bars, Basic Building Materials, most components
    Titanium Gray rocky veins near the starting area and north/northeast The other half of BBM (1 Ti + 1 W → 10 BBM); sheets, rods, mid-game gear
    Calcium White tooth-like formations in damp terrain, north/east Glass, chemicals, Calcium Blocks
    Helium-3 Purple gas rocks and rare purple geysers (far north/east); needs the Helium-3 Extractor (Selenian Lv6) Mid-game refining, fuel, Furnace recipes
    Sulfur Bright yellow corrosive zones that damage you on contact; no loose chunks Late-game production — but only minable right after a Rupture with the Sulfur Extractor (Selenian Lv9)
    Ignitium Spawns on the surface after Rupture waves The best Data Point farm in the game (~2,000 DP per stack of 5)

    A few rules of thumb: drop an Ore Excavator on every ore patch you plan to use long-term — it mines automatically and needs only power. Pure nodes (solid single-ore deposits) can run up to 8× extractor speed, so build your factory near them rather than around mixed deposits.

    Building Your First Base

    Location matters more than base design in the early game. The safest early spot is southwest, near your Orbital Lander, around the clustered Titanium and Wolfram deposits. Build against a cliff face or rock formation so natural terrain covers one or two sides against bug attacks. A plateau east of the starting crater (near a lake with helium/sulfur fields) is the second-best option if you want more room to expand.

    When you place buildings, follow the Golden Rules:

    • Build on platforms, not bare ground. Lay platform decks first — they make rail routing and expansion ten times easier. Press Y to lock height alignment, and add support pillars where a tile reports "not stable."
    • Elevate buildings slightly with the mouse wheel so they don't clip terrain and you leave clearance for rails underneath.
    • Read the lights. Structure lights are a health check: blue = operating normally, red = underpowered grid, yellow = configuration issue (missing recipe or input). A red base is almost always a power connection problem, not a production problem.
    • Plant more Base Cores to claim outposts around rich ore nodes. Each core extends your buildable area.

    Automation 101: The Core Loop

    The whole game is one conveyor-fed chain, and it doesn't get more complicated than this:

    Ore Excavator → Rails → Smelter → Rails → Fabricator → Storage / Orbital Cargo Launcher

    • Ore Excavator — auto-mines Titanium, Wolfram, and Calcium deposits. Place it on a flat ore patch and feed it power.
    • Rails (v.1) — the conveyor belt. Use Rail Supports to bridge gaps and Rail Connectors to merge several excavators into one smelter. Keep paths short to avoid bottlenecks.
    • Smelter — turns ore into bars: 2 ore → 2 bars, about 60/min (a 2-second cycle). The Calcium Block recipe unlocks once you've mined a lot of Calcium.
    • Fabricator — the value-add machine: bars in, components out (sheets, plates, beams, rods, wires, and the advanced parts). Its most important recipe is 1 Titanium + 1 Wolfram → 10 Basic Building Materials.
    • Orbital Cargo Launcher (OCL) — the endpoint. Feed it refined products and it launches them to the orbital corporations, earning you reputation and tech unlocks. A full automated line Smelter → Fabricator → OCL runs itself once powered.

    One more station: the Furnace (Selenian Lv4) handles high-temperature work — Glass (2 Calcium + 1 Helium-3), powders, and inductors. The Assembler (Selenian Lv10) is the end-game station for the most complex components.

    Power is the real bottleneck. Solar generators (day) and wind turbines (later) must be connected to your machines — usually through the platform/rail grid. If a machine isn't running, check power before you touch recipes.

    The Five Corporations: What to Level First

    Every building and recipe in the game is gated behind five corporations, each with its own level track. (A sixth track, Training, only appears during the tutorial — it unlocks your very first Solar Generator, Ore Excavator, Rails, and Smelter, then steps aside for the real five.) You level them by exporting materials via the Orbital Cargo Launcher (the scalable, long-term method) or by spending Data Points at the Corporate Terminal (fast but wasteful early — save DP for recipes).

    Corporation Focus Headline unlocks
    Moon Energy Power & mapping Recipe Station (Lv2), Map (Lv3), Wind Turbine
    Selenian Materials & mining Base Core, Fabricator (Lv2), Furnace (Lv4), Helium-3 Extractor (Lv6), Refinery & Sulfur Extractor (Lv9), Assembler (Lv10)
    Clever Robotics Logistics & automation Orbital Cargo Launcher, Rails (v.1–v.3), Storage Depot, Building Drone (Lv4), Cargo Dispatcher/Receiver (Lv7)
    Griffits Blue Combat & defense Platforms, UPP-7 Pistol (Lv2), Grenades (Lv3), Defense Turret (Lv6), Defense Tower (Lv9)
    Future Health Solutions Survival & food Habitat & modules, Regeneration Chamber (Lv2), Medtool (Lv3), Food Station (Lv6), Compounder (Lv9)

    The recommended early path (works for every seed):

    1. Everything to Level 2 first — it's cheap and unlocks the basics of every track.
    2. Moon Energy → Level 3 for the Map. There's no mini-map at the start; pressing M does nothing until you've earned this. It's the single biggest quality-of-life unlock.
    3. Selenian → Level 2 for the Fabricator.
    4. Griffits Blue → Level 2 for the UPP-7 Pistol — you'll need it long before you think you will.
    5. After that, push Clever Robotics → Level 4 for the Building Drone, which gives you an aerial view for placing rails and buildings.

    Data Points & Research

    Data Points (DP) are the game's research currency. You earn them by feeding items into the Analyzing Station, which converts eligible inventory items into DP (it needs a power connection — roughly 30 kW).

    The best DP sources, ranked:

    • Ignitium (post-Rupture) — a stack of 5 converts to ~2,000 DP. The best farm in the game.
    • Old tech / wreckage / computer parts — from abandoned bases and dead bodies; up to ~400 DP each, but one-time use.
    • Biological samples — the Goliath vermin drops a sample worth 5,000 DP.
    • Glowcap — cave mushrooms that appear after Ruptures; ~90 DP each, renewable every cycle.
    • Meteorite Hearts & rare plants — decent value, but you'll also need them for crafting, so think before analyzing.

    A full post-Rupture cave run — grab Glowcap on the way down, scoop Ignitium on the way back — routinely nets 5,000+ DP.

    Where to spend DP: the Recipe Station (Moon Energy Lv2) is where recipes get unlocked. Pick a station tab (Smelter, Fabricator, Furnace, Food Station…), then spend the required materials plus Data Points to unlock its recipes. Don't dump DP into corporations — level those by launching exports instead, and save DP for recipes.

    Blueprints: advanced recipes need Blueprints, found in Blue Chests inside Abandoned Settlements scattered across the planet (expect Vermin and Rupture risk at each one). Get the Map before blueprint hunting — you'll waste hours wandering otherwise.

    Combat: Surviving the Vermins

    Every hostile creature on Arcadia-7 is an alien bug, collectively called Vermins. They swarm, they respawn after every Rupture, and they will absolutely end an exploration trip if you're not ready.

    Enemy What it does How to deal with it
    Flinger Ranged scorpion-like bug, lobs toxic blobs in an arc Dodge the blob, close distance, finish with a shotgun
    Slasher Fast, black-widow-like leaper; its mouth/back is the weak spot Close-range shotgun blast, or scope its glowing red eyes
    Younger Slasher Small grey-trimmed version, no armor One shot from most rifles puts it down
    Exploder Blue bug with a growing sac — explodes, disables your shield, and its fog buffs other Vermins Shoot the sac from range before it reaches you — top priority in any swarm
    Spitter Small green ranged bug, spits toxic matter Pop its sac while it's stationary
    Goliath The boss — armored front, can shield itself, surprisingly fast Only the unprotected backside takes damage. Wait for its charge, then unload into its rear. Solo players: use high ground. Drops a Biological Sample worth 5,000 DP. Spawns near the Sulfur Pits, northeast

    Weapon progression: your first gun is the UPP-7 Pistol (Griffits Blue Lv2). Upgrade to the MAR-9 "Phantom" assault rifle for swarm-clearing and the SLAMS-12 shotgun for close-range work and Goliath weak spots. Grenades cull swarms fast. Later, Defense Turrets and Defense Towers automate base defense — but they eat an ammo belt, so keep your ammo production line running or they'll go silent mid-wave.

    Combat rules to live by: enemies respawn after every planetary regeneration, so always carry lots of ammo — you can burn 500+ rounds on one exploration trip, and ammo is cheap to craft. On death you drop everything you're carrying, and any gun except the pistol is destroyed — you respawn at your Orbital Lander or the last Regeneration Chamber you activated.

    The one friendly bug: Grubbler — tiny, edible, non-hostile, found in twisted gold-tinted trees on ridges and the mid-eastern part of the map (more appear after Ruptures). They're the base ingredient for gels: Calorie Gel (+65 calories), Aqua Gel, Nutri Gel, Antidote Gel, and Termo Res Plasm.

    Co-op, Characters & Settings

    • Co-op (up to 4 players) is genuinely the best way to start. One player builds while another scavenges; you die less, learn faster, and Goliaths become trivial with someone tanking the front.
    • Character choice doesn't matter mechanically. All four characters play identically — only dialogue differs. You can switch later at a Regeneration Chamber.
    • Saves live at C:\Users\USERNAME\AppData\Local\StarRupture\Saved\SaveGames — worth backing up before big updates.
    • It's Early Access. Balance shifts between patches. If something here disagrees with your in-game UI, trust the UI and the patch notes.

    Pro Tips

    • Never run out of the emergency kit. 1 Meteor Heart + 100 BBM + an airlock saves your entire inventory more times than you'd think.
    • Vent your mining heat early. Press R just before the heat bar caps — you lose almost no mining time, and overheat cooldowns are painfully long.
    • Build on platforms, always. Rebuilding a foundation is ten minutes of work; replanning a whole factory around terrain is an afternoon.
    • Connect power before you blame recipes. Red lights = underpowered grid. Most "broken" early factories are just missing a generator link.
    • Keep the map unlock as a hard priority. Wandering without the map is the #1 beginner time sink.
    • Do a post-Rupture cave loop for Data Points. One Glowcap + Ignitium run sets up your whole first research burst.
    • Exports, not DP, for corporations. The Orbital Cargo Launcher leveling is free once your lines run — save every Data Point for recipes.
    • Try co-op for the first session. The game is good solo, but four prisoners on Arcadia-7 is where it shines.

    FAQ

    Q: How long does a Rupture cycle take? The world cycles between calm, unstable, and chaotic states, with a full eruption every several in-game cycles. You always get two warnings and a 15-second countdown before it hits — the danger is exploration, not ambush.

    Q: What's the first thing I should build? A Solar Generator, then an Ore Excavator on a Titanium or Wolfram patch. Power first, automation second.

    Q: Should I spend Data Points on corporations? No. Level corporations by exporting materials through the Orbital Cargo Launcher. Spend Data Points at the Recipe Station — that's where they count.

    Q: What's the best early base location? Southwest, near the Orbital Lander, against a cliff, beside clustered Titanium and Wolfram. The plateau east of the starting crater is the runner-up.

    Q: How do I stop dying to Ruptures while exploring? Carry the emergency kit (1 Meteor Heart + 100+ BBM) and drop an emergency Habitat + airlock the second the countdown starts. Everything refunds at 100% when you deconstruct.

    Q: Is the game worth playing in Early Access? If you like factory games, yes — the automation loop is solid and the Mostly Positive rating backs it up. If you want polished UI and complete content, a few major updates might suit you better. It's also on sale more often than most EA titles.

    Q: How does StarRupture compare to Satisfactory / Factorio? It's the survival-ier cousin of both: Satisfactory gives you a friendlier 3D world with less pressure, Factorio gives you deeper optimization with no survival layer — StarRupture puts a survival and combat layer on top of the factory grind. See the game comparison for how all three stack up.
